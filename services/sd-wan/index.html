// SD-WAN, explained

One network for all your branches, managed from one screen

SD-WAN connects your offices, stores or plants over any internet link (leased line, broadband or 4G/5G) and manages them as one private network. Traffic between sites is encrypted, and you control it from a single dashboard.

The useful part is what happens when a link has trouble. SD-WAN keeps checking every connection and moves calls, billing or ERP traffic to the healthier one automatically. Staff usually don't notice anything.

How to choose an SD-WAN service provider in India

SD-WAN devices from most vendors do similar things. What decides how well it works for you is the links at each branch, who runs the network after go-live and how faults get fixed. Ask about these before you sign.

  1. 01

    Links at every branch

    SD-WAN is only as good as the connections under it. Ask the provider to check, address by address, which carriers can give a leased line or broadband there, and where a 4G/5G SIM will have to do for now.

  2. 02

    Two different paths

    Failover only helps if the two links don't share the same cable or carrier. For important branches, ask for the primary and backup on different networks.

  3. 03

    Managed or self-run

    Find out who writes the traffic rules, who watches the links at night and who calls the carrier when a fibre is cut. A managed service should cover all three.

  4. 04

    Rules for your applications

    Billing, POS, ERP and voice should be prioritised over video streaming and updates. Ask to see how these rules will be set for your business before rollout.

  5. 05

    Security at the branch

    Check whether the device includes a firewall and web filtering, or whether each site still needs a separate security box.

  6. 06

    Working with MPLS

    If you already run MPLS, the provider should be able to connect SD-WAN branches to it, so you can move sites over in stages instead of all at once.
